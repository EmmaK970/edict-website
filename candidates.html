Hasnat Sujon

Doctoral Candidate – Project 13

📍 University of Galway

Hasnat is working on developing a guideline for culturally appropriate dissemination of clinical trials (EDICT Project # 13), under the supervision of Dr. Sinéad Hynes and Dr. Eimear Morrissey at the University of Galway, Ireland.

Hasnat is a medical doctor and a clinical trialist with ~10 years' experience leading the set-up, execution, and close-out of 10+ clinical trials (Phase II–III; vaccines, infectious disease) across multi-country settings, working with industry sponsors (Moderna, GSK) and academic investigators (NIH, WHO, Johns Hopkins). He completed an MSc in Clinical Trials from the London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine (Commonwealth Scholar) and an MPH (Epidemiology) from University College Dublin. He has published in leading peer-reviewed journals and has secured competitive research funding from NIH, Sida, and WHO.

Maeve Herlihy

Doctoral Candidate – Project 10

📍 UMC Utrecht

Maeve holds a BSc in Public Health Sciences from University College Cork and an MSc in Epidemiology from the London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine. Her research experience spans both quantitative and qualitative methods. She previously worked at the HRB Clinical Research Facility at University College Cork, where her work focused on clinical trial design tools, reporting guidelines and pragmatic trial design principles. She was also part of the PRECIS-3 research team, a trial design tool aimed at helping trialists match design decisions to the intended purpose of a trial.

Maeve will be working on Project 10, Methodological and Operational Tools for Enhancing EDI in Clinical Trials, as part of Work Package 3. Her research will focus on evaluating approaches to enhance equity, diversity and inclusion (EDI) in clinical trials and developing practical guidance to support their use.

Teena Thomas

Doctoral Candidate – Project 1

📍 Catholic University of Croatia

Teena Thomas is the doctoral candidate on EDICT Project 1, based at the Catholic University of Croatia in Zagreb, under the mentorship of Prof. Livia Puljak and the supervision of Prof. Damir Sapunar. Her project, part of Work Package 1, examines how equity, diversity and inclusion are planned and realised across European clinical trials. Drawing on trial registries and publications from 1993 onwards, she will compare single- and multi-country trials and track how EDI practices have changed over time.

Before joining EDICT, Teena worked as a Research Assistant at the Centre for Health Research Methodology, University of Galway, on the HRB-TMRN-funded QASSIST study (Qualitative Research Supporting Inclusive Trials). The study explores how qualitative research can inform the design and conduct of inclusive clinical trials. Earlier in her career, she spent two years in healthcare market research across the Asia-Pacific region. She holds a Master’s in Health Data Science, an MBA in Healthcare Management and a BSc in Microbiology-Biochemistry.

Anh Tran Nhat Phuong

Doctoral Candidate – Project 5

📍 Paris Cité University

I am a doctoral researcher and Marie Skłodowska-Curie fellow in the EDICT Doctoral Network, based in the METHODS team at the Centre of Research in Epidemiology and Statistics (CRESS, UMR 1153), Université Paris Cité. After training as a medical doctor in Vietnam, I gained clinical research experience at University Medical Center Ho Chi Minh City and OUCRU Vietnam. I then completed an MSc in Epidemiology at the University of Antwerp (Belgium). My thesis applied target trial emulation to occupational health cohort data from Vietnam, where, as in many low- and middle-income countries, research databases are scarce. This experience shaped my interest in using causal inference and real-world data to generate evidence for populations underrepresented in clinical trials. In EDICT Project 5, supervised by Prof. Isabelle Boutron, Dr. Sally Yaacoub and Dr. Neil O'Leary (University of Galway), I am assessing whether target trials can be emulated using real-world data sources such as CONSTANCES and SNDS. I will also identify barriers and possible solutions, and develop a consensus-based framework to support decision-making for underrepresented groups in trials.
